# Volunteer tools in 2026: where they fall short

Research note, October 24, 2026. Numbers in brackets are sources. **(not verified)** marks a claim with one weak source or none.

## Summary of gaps

- **Approved people sit idle.** The tools publish a board and wait for volunteers to pick from it. In the documentation I read, none drafts work for a person nobody has asked. One coordinator found applicants who had waited over a year [34]. The NHS program had 600,000 vetted people and about 100,000 tasks [49].
- **Requirements sit on the person, not on approval per role.** Most tools store a qualification on the person and let an activity require it [22][25]. No tool I found documents a team lead approving people for their team's work.
- **Gating is manual or costs more.** VolunteerHub gates by moving people between groups by hand [23]. Golden Credentials (August 2026) is a paid add-on [27]. Rosterfy automates it per role at about $7,000 a year [17][30].
- **Manual overrides skip the rules.** In Galaxy Digital, a manager who adds a person to a need "bypass[es] the qualifications" [26].
- **A volunteer's record does not travel.** POINT background checks are not shared with other nonprofits [29]. Better Impact keeps each organization's database "completely independent" [33].
- **Price grows with the volunteer list** at Civic Champs, Better Impact, Volgistics and Track It Forward [8][12][13][14]. A large, mostly quiet bench costs money.
- **Hour logging is the center of the workflow.** Tools sell approval queues and reports on who is behind [43]. 26% of UK volunteers say volunteering is becoming too much like paid work [48].
- **Coordinators are stretched.** 29% of UK volunteer managers in one survey report burnout [41]. Learning curve is the top G2 complaint about Better Impact [38].

**What small groups use instead.** In a 2015 buyer survey, 63% used Excel, Google Drive and similar tools [50]. In a 2025 UK survey, only 44% of programs had invested in volunteer systems [42]. Mutual aid organizers on WhatsApp reported "overwhelming numbers of requests" and volunteers leaving from overload [45]. A shelter coordinator called juggling tools "hectic and hard to keep straight" [37].

## Evidence for each gap

### Approved but never given work

A March 2026 Better Impact post describes a coordinator with more willing people than roles, who onboarded the newest first. "A handful of people had been sitting on that spreadsheet waiting to volunteer for over a year" [34]. VolunteerHub, June 2026: when one coordinator does all follow-up, "some sign-ups wait too long for a response, and others may not receive one at all" [35]. A 2016 article reports that 20% of people applying through Do-It never heard back, and that half of enquiries in Scotland had no reply within three weeks [36].

The NHS Volunteer Responders case is already in [ACTIVATION-PRD.md](../ACTIVATION-PRD.md): 750,000 applied, 600,000 passed vetting, and about 100,000 tasks were logged by early May 2020 [49].

### Requirements and per-role approval

- Better Impact is the most complete. An activity can require several qualifications, with minimum levels and optional expiry. Expired items block assignment. Volunteers can submit items for admin approval. Permissions sit on the qualification, so one rule covers every activity [22].
- Bloomerang Volunteer keeps qualifications at the organization level or for one opportunity [25]. This is the closest thing to per-opportunity approval I found.
- VolunteerHub: an admin adds a trained person to an "approved" group, and only that group can register. No expiry or automatic removal is described [23]. Blocking new users from events can mean emailing support [24].
- Galaxy Digital: manual adds skip unmet qualifications. "The site allows this, but the organization should understand they are doing so" [26].
- Golden: since April 2026, an opportunity sets who can find it separately from who can register [51]. Credentials send notices when items expire, but the newsletter does not say expiry blocks sign-up [27].
- Rosterfy: onboarding differs by role, such as a license for drivers. It alerts before credentials lapse [30]. This is vendor copy.

I found no product that documents team-level approvers, such as a radio show producer approving people for one show. **(Absence of evidence, not proof.)**

### Requirement records that do not travel

POINT: "If you volunteer with two nonprofits that both require screenings, you would need to complete both" [29]. Better Impact lets one MyImpactPage login join many organizations. Each organization's data stays separate, and hours are reported by organization [33]. Golden lets a check be reused across Golden opportunities, but the volunteer pays for it and it lasts one year [28]. In UK research on volunteer passports, people most wanted portable ID and DBS checks, to cut repeated onboarding [44].

### Cost for small groups

The common basis is the size of the volunteer list: active volunteers (Civic Champs [12]), accepted plus inactive profiles (Better Impact [8]), records (Bloomerang [10], Volgistics [13]), or users (Track It Forward [14]). VolunteerHub adds a setup fee [9]. Free plans (Golden [11], POINT [16], SignUpGenius [15]) center on events and slots. A Capterra reviewer calls SignUpGenius ads "distracting" [39].

### Hour logging and chasing

Vendors sell hour approval, bulk approval and reports on "which approvers are behind" [43]. That suggests backlogs are common, but it is vendor copy. I found no independent study of time spent on hours **(not verified)**.

### Coordinator capacity and turnover

29% of UK volunteer managers report burnout and 28% call their role's expectations unrealistic, in a Rosterfy survey of 300 managers published as sponsored content [41][42]. G2 lists "Difficult Learning" and "Not User-Friendly" as Better Impact's top complaint themes [38], and reviewers find Volgistics reports "cumbersome" [40]. I found no figure for coordinator turnover **(not verified)**.

## Research figures on retention

- **United States.** 28.3% of people 16 and older volunteered through an organization in the year to September 2023. Average hours per volunteer fell from 96.5 in 2017 to 70 in 2023 [46].
- **Year to year.** Census data from 2002 to 2006 showed 31% of baby boomer volunteers did not return the next year [47]. No current figure for all ages was found.
- **UK.** 77% of recent volunteers were likely to continue in 2022, down from 80% in 2018. 26% felt their volunteering was becoming too much like paid work, up from 19%. 24% felt expectations were unreasonable. Only 10% of those stopping blamed how the group was run. Most cited less time [48].

## What I could not check

Reddit blocked access (403), and searches surfaced no r/nonprofit or r/volunteer threads. I found no sign that r/VolunteerCoordinators exists. G2 and Capterra pages were blocked, so review quotes come from search extracts. Galaxy Digital prices are not public. I found no documentation of gating in Volgistics or VolunteerMatters.
