# How small organizations talk about their people: research for scoping membership

**Date:** November 25, 2026
**Asked by:** the owner, who said membership shouldn't assume paid memberships, and asked for research on "the sorts of language that people are using around their struggles with broader membership engagement, membership organization structure" before scoping further.

## Short answer

Small groups rarely describe their problem as "membership management". They describe four things:

1. **The same few people do everything.** Reliable people get handed more until they burn out, and newcomers find it hard to get involved.
2. **Nobody knows who's doing what, or who did it last year.** Leaders change every one to three years, and the list, the logins and the know-how leave with them.
3. **Too many tools, each priced per person.** A spreadsheet, a group chat, a sign-up sheet, a mailing list and an email tool, each with its own account and its own bill that grows with the group.
4. **People on the list who aren't really involved.** Association surveys rank this first, and it's the most common reason members don't renew.

Dues and renewals matter to paid associations, but they are one case. Many groups have members who never pay: clubs, mutual aid groups, neighborhood associations, alumni chapters and parent groups. For them, "member" means someone who belongs and might help.

## Method and limits

- Twelve web searches on November 25, 2026, aimed at small nonprofits, clubs, associations, mutual aid groups, parent groups and neighborhood associations.
- Reddit refused automated access, so this has no forum threads in people's own words. Most sources are surveys, practitioner guides and vendor blogs. Vendor sources are marked, because they sell a fix for the problem they describe.
- One first-hand account: a text message to the owner from someone who runs a small organization, used with the owner's knowledge and summarized here without their name.
- No interviews. The next step below is a short round of them.

## What people say

### 1. The same few people do everything

- Guides for small clubs describe the same cycle: dependable volunteers get more tasks over time until the work sits with a handful of people ([Cheddar Up](https://www.cheddarup.com/blog/volunteer-burnout-prevention/), vendor). A swim-club guide adds that newcomers find getting involved daunting ([Swimly](https://swimly.uk/blog/volunteer-burnout-in-swim-clubs/), vendor).
- The fixes they suggest are small, one-off jobs that are easy to say yes to, and writing down who owns what ([Swimly](https://swimly.uk/blog/volunteer-burnout-in-swim-clubs/); [Swoop](https://swoopcommunity.com.au/guides/avoiding-volunteer-burnout/), vendor).
- Sports clubs list feeling overburdened, unappreciated or disorganized as the reasons volunteers quit ([Club Volunteer](https://clubvolunteer.com.au/blog/volunteer-retention-sports-clubs), vendor).
- Chapters "recycle their volunteer leaders until they burn out because no one is waiting in the wings" ([Engage Journal](http://engagejournal.org/VolunteerEngagementCha-Cha)).
- A VolunteerPro report, as cited by Fionta, puts active volunteers per association at 1 to 25, down from 100 to 250 before the pandemic ([Fionta](https://fionta.com/insights/reimagine-volunteering-and-leadership-to-increase-member-engagement/)). This is secondhand and the method isn't stated.
- Mutual aid groups name burnout and unclear structure as the most common reasons they fail, and recommend rotating roles, including who handles intake ([Lefty Magazine](https://leftymagazine.com/articles/mutual-aid-burnout-lessons-from/); [AlwaysReadyHQ](https://www.alwaysreadyhq.com/9605/lessons-learned-from-mutual-aid-initiatives/)).

This is the problem the tool was first built for: work goes to whoever has waited longest, and short tasks come first.

### 2. Turnover takes the list and the know-how with it

- Boards are usually volunteers serving one to three years, with "little to no onboarding", so "knowledge is constantly lost and rebuilt" ([BuildingLink](https://www.buildinglink.io/blog/board-member-turnover-how-to-protect-institutional-knowledge-with-better-document-management), vendor).
- A typical gap: a new treasurer doesn't know where the accounts are or what payments run automatically ([Board Beacon](https://boardbeaconhq.com/blog/volunteer-board-turnover-guide/), vendor).
- For parent groups: "PTA email breaks because the tools are tied to people, not to the organization" ([Mailsoftly](https://mailsoftly.com/blog/pta-communications-volunteer-turnover/), vendor).

The language here is about roles and handoffs: who holds which job, since when, and what they know.

### 3. Too many tools, priced per person

- In Omatic's 2026 survey of more than 800 nonprofits, 70% run five or more core platforms, up from 62% a year earlier ([GlobeNewswire](https://www.globenewswire.com/news-release/2026/04/08/3270467/0/en/Omatic-Releases-Fourth-Annual-Nonprofit-Technology-Ecosystem-Trends-Report-Uncovers-a-Sector-Drowning-in-Data-And-the-Struggle-to-Trust-It.html)). The sample leans toward larger organizations.
- A TechSoup Canada deck lists "too many accounts" as a pain point for busy nonprofits ([SlideShare](https://www.slideshare.net/slideshow/10-online-tools-for-busy-nonprofits/44001061)).
- Pricing that grows with the group: WildApricot counts every contact toward its tier, including past members and newsletter subscribers. A competitor reports its 100-contact plan rose from about $40 to $66 a month ([Orgo](https://orgo.space/alternatives/wild-apricot/), competitor; [Somiti](https://somiti.net/blog/wild-apricot-too-expensive), competitor).
- Google Workspace is free for qualifying nonprofits, but an upgrade moves every user to the same paid plan ([Google](https://knowledge.workspace.google.com/admin/billing/upgrade-from-google-workspace-for-nonprofits)).
- Informal groups put urgent coordination in Signal or WhatsApp, records in Google Drive, and shifts in SignUpGenius ([AlwaysReadyHQ](https://www.alwaysreadyhq.com/9719/strategies-for-forming-local-mutual-aid-groups/)).

**The first-hand account** fits this pattern. The person keeps email, files, newsletter design, project management, bookkeeping and volunteer management in separate tools. They want "the functionality of basecamp and Google workspace all in one that is paid in one lump sum instead of per user."

### 4. People on the list who aren't really involved

- Michigan association executives named "keeping members and volunteers actively involved" their most common concern in 2025 ([MSAE](https://www.msae.org/blog/top-5-pressing-concerns-executives)).
- In the Association Universe survey, member engagement ranked first among challenges, and lack of engagement was the top reason members gave for not renewing ([MemberJungle](https://www.memberjungle.com/blog/membership-survey-results--lessons-we-can-learn-from-them), vendor).
- A global report puts the share of associations calling engagement a top concern at 85% ([Memnet](https://www.memnet.com.au/post/purpose-under-pressure-global-association-trends-in-2025)).
- Members "who've never experienced a personal connection" with staff or other members rarely volunteer ([Fionta](https://fionta.com/insights/reimagine-volunteering-and-leadership-to-increase-member-engagement/)). About a quarter of respondents in one association survey said turning away would-be volunteers had made some members less engaged ([SAME](https://www.same.org/wp-content/uploads/2025/01/enclosure-2.pdf)).

These sources are association executives. Small groups without staff say the same thing in plainer words: getting people involved, people showing up, the same faces.

## The words

| People say | Avoid |
|---|---|
| get people involved, help out, show up, pitch in | activation, mobilize, deploy |
| the same few people, the regulars, the core group | power users, high-engagement segment |
| roster, list, our people, members | contacts, records, constituents |
| who's doing what, roles, board, committee, chair | workforce, capacity allocation |
| hand off, pass it on, when I step down | knowledge management, succession pipeline |
| group chat, sign-up sheet, spreadsheet | (these are the tools Volley replaces, and the words to meet people with) |

"Engagement" is how association executives and vendors talk. Small groups mostly say "involved".

## What this means for the scope

These are recommendations for the owner, not decisions.

1. **Membership without dues should be the default.** In the current build, a person's state comes from an expiry date. A person with no expiry date is simply a member, so dues-free groups already work. But the home page, the settings and the person page lead with renewals and dues. Recommendation: an organization setting, "We track renewals and dues", off by default. When it's off, the expiry and dues fields, renewal settings and renewal copy are hidden.
2. **Lead with getting more people involved, not running a membership.** The strongest shared pain is the same few people doing everything. That is what the tool does best, and it holds whether or not anyone pays dues. Headline candidates for the owner: "Get more of your people involved." or "Stop relying on the same few people."
3. **Add roles with terms.** Who holds which job (chair, treasurer, committee member, team captain), since when and until when. It answers the turnover problem, it is small to build on top of teams, and it makes the roster belong to the organization instead of to one person's spreadsheet.
4. **Who hasn't been involved lately is in tension with an earlier decision.** The pasted plan wanted to show "who hasn't been involved recently". Decision 0011 dropped screens about people who drifted away, because they point toward chasing people. A gentle version would show it only to coordinators, as a list of people never asked, which the People screen already has. The owner should decide.
5. **Don't chase the all-in-one.** Bookkeeping, file storage, project management and newsletter design are each a product with strong free or cheap options. The useful part of the first-hand account is the pricing and the count of tools: one free tool that replaces the spreadsheet, the sign-up sheet and the group-chat asks is a fair promise. Replacing Google Workspace is not.
6. **Volley's place is different from membership databases.** The owner corrected the agent's first reading on November 25. Free tiers on paid products, such as Spond, TeamLinkt, MemberPlanet and Raklet, aren't a free product: they cap groups and charge when the group grows ([Capterra](https://www.capterra.com/membership-management-software/s/free/)). And the organizations Volley is for don't use those tools at all. Membership databases keep records; they don't get people involved. Volley does: it asks the right people for each job, spreads the work past the regulars, and shows who has never been asked. The launch copy should lead with that.

## What the owner decided

On November 26 the owner approved recommendations 1 to 3, changed 4, and kept 5 and 6 as launch rules. See [decision 0015](../decisions/0015-roles-and-succession.md).

- Renewals and dues are optional, so formal and informal organizations both fit.
- The messaging leads with participation, but keeps "membership management" and "volunteer management" as the categories people search for.
- Roles with terms, including past holders, vacancies and terms ending soon.
- Nobody is labeled inactive. The product shows what each person was offered, accepted and took part in.
- No all-in-one suite before launch. Customer interviews explore it.
- Free stays an acquisition strategy, not the only selling point.
- New: succession. When a leader leaves, the role keeps its responsibilities, open tasks and history, so the next person sees what they inherit.

The owner also named the larger opportunity: distributing responsibility across a membership. "Having 5,000 members means relatively little if the same 25 people do everything."

## Customer discovery

Compare three kinds of organization instead of a mixed sample:

- **Large membership, small staff:** statewide athletic associations, coaches associations and professional societies. Commercially the most interesting: thousands of members, set budgets and heavy coordination. They can use the free product now and pay for automation later.
- **Rotating volunteer leadership:** PTAs, professional chapters, neighborhood associations and amateur sports clubs.
- **Recurring operational demands:** festivals, food distribution programs and community service organizations.

Ask each to show their real artifacts: spreadsheets, committee rosters, recruitment emails and leadership handoff documents. They show the work better than a question about which features people want.

Questions to ask alongside the artifacts:

- Where does your list of people live today, and who has the login?
- The last time something needed doing, how did you find someone? How long did it take?
- Who does most of the work? What would it take for someone new to do part of it?
- What happened the last time a leader stepped down?
- Do your members pay anything? If they do, how do you know who has?

## What this research doesn't show

Much of the evidence comes from vendors and practitioner publications. It shows the problems recur. It doesn't show how often organizations have them together, or whether they would adopt a new product to solve them. The launch tests that. After launch, product decisions should come from what organizations do in the product, more than from further research.
